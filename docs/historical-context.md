# Historical Context for Design

## Setting & Period

*Monna Vanna* is set during a **late 15th-century siege of Pisa by Florence**, a historical period rich in visual and architectural texture. However, **the characters and plot are entirely Maeterlinck's invention** — this is not a historical drama or biopic.

## What Is Historical

**The siege atmosphere and setting:**

- **Late 15th-century Tuscany** — The period's architecture, costume, and material culture inform the visual design
- **Pisan-Florentine conflict** — Real wars occurred between these city-states during this era
- **Siege warfare** — Starvation, mercenary armies, city walls, and political intrigue are historically grounded

**Design elements drawn from history:**

- Tuscan Renaissance architecture (Pisa's Romanesque, Florence's Quattrocento)
- Period-appropriate costume (1470s–1490s Italian dress)
- Candlelit interiors, torch-lit battlements, pre-gunpowder siege equipment
- Social hierarchy: Seigniory (city council), condottieri (mercenary captains), citizen militia

## What Is Maeterlinck's Fiction

**The principal characters are invented:**

- **Giovanna (Monna Vanna)** — Not a historical figure
- **Guido Colonna** — Not a historical figure
- **Prinzivalle** — Not a historical figure
- **Marco Colonna** — Not a historical figure
- **Trivulzio, Vedio, Borso, Torello** — Not historical figures

**The plot is symbolic drama, not historical event:**

- Maeterlinck uses the siege as a **moral pressure cooker**, not a documentary recreation
- The play is about **sacrifice, loyalty, trust, and moral ambiguity**, not about actual Pisan-Florentine politics
- The famous mantle scene (Act II) is theatrical invention, not historical incident

## Design Approach

**We borrow historical texture, not historical accuracy:**

1. **Architecture & Costume:**  
   Use late 15th-century Tuscan visual language (Ghirlandaio, Botticelli, Gozzoli) to ground the world, but prioritize **dramatic clarity** over archaeological precision.

2. **Social Roles:**  
   Respect the hierarchy (city leaders, mercenary captains, soldiers, citizens), but focus on **moral tension**, not political minutiae.

3. **Material Culture:**  
   Torches, candles, wool cloaks, stone walls, siege rations — these create **atmosphere and stakes**, not documentary detail.

## Not a Biopic, Not a History Lesson

**Monna Vanna is a moral fable set in a plausible historical world.**

- Do not research "the real Monna Vanna" — she didn't exist
- Do not fact-check Maeterlinck's invented sieges or battles
- Do not try to map the play's events onto actual Pisan-Florentine chronology

**The historical setting is a canvas for Maeterlinck's symbolic drama.**

Think of it like:
- Shakespeare's *Julius Caesar* (historical setting, but the psychology and dialogue are Shakespeare's invention)
- Arthur Miller's *The Crucible* (Salem witch trials as backdrop for moral allegory)

## Visual References

**For architectural and costume texture, study:**

- **Pisa:** Romanesque cathedral, Leaning Tower, city walls, piazzas
- **Florence:** Palazzo Vecchio, Bargello, Medici palaces, Orsanmichele
- **Painting:** Ghirlandaio's Florentine interiors, Gozzoli's *Procession of the Magi*, Botticelli's early work

**Do not study:**

- Modern Pisa tourism photos (wrong palette, wrong light)
- Hollywood sword-and-sandal epics (wrong tone)
- Fantasy concept art (too modern, too glossy)

## Tone

**Late 15th-century Tuscany = moral seriousness + material restraint.**

- Stone, wool, iron, candlelight
- Dignity under pressure
- Nobility without sentimentality
- Heroic staging, not naturalistic clutter

This is the world Maeterlinck asks us to imagine, and it's the world the Tempera Chronicle style was designed to evoke.
