# Act I: The Price — Units (Sutro 1904)

**Source lock:** Alfred Sutro, London: George Allen, 1904 — https://archive.org/details/monnavanna00maetiala
**Rule:** Word-for-word. Every Sutro line is spoken. No paraphrase, no cuts, no invented dialogue.
**Style:** Tempera Chronicle (`docs/style-tempera-chronicle.md`).
**Mantle visuals:** Cloak throat-to-floor; implication only. Sutro's words about the mantle stay intact.

## Director's correction to `plan/trilogy.md`

The current Act I beats in `plan/trilogy.md` **do not match Sutro**. Fix on the next repo pass. Facts from the 1904 text:

- Act I is **one continuous scene** in **a room in the palace of Guido Colonna** (window on the country; door at back; marble columns). Not a multi-location night walk as the primary act.
- **Marco**, not Borso, is the envoy. Guido sent his father to Prinzivalle. Marco *returns* and reports.
- Prinzivalle's offer (via Marco): a convoy of food, wine, cattle, powder, lead **this night**, if they **deliver Giovanna**; she **returns at first dawn**; token of victory: she come **alone, clad only in her mantle**.
- The **Seigniory has already refused** to owe safety to the sacrifice of a woman's love; they **summoned Vanna** and placed the city's fate in her hands.
- **Vedio is not in Act I** (Prinzivalle's secretary — Act II). Principals here: Guido, Marco, Vanna, Borso, Torello; crowd at the door at her entrance.
- Vanna's decision line is not a speech about "one woman's dignity is a small price." Her first spoken beat is: **"My father, I shall go to-night."**

## Staging approach (long speeches)

One room. Treat it as a **filmed performance with cinematic coverage**, not a montage that skips talk.

- **A-cam master:** locked or slow-creep wide of the palace room (window, table, columns, door).
- **B-cam speaker / C-cam listener:** hold faces through entire speeches; cut on thought, not to "save time."
- **Inserts (never replacing lines):** window/breach, empty powder mood, Marco's lame step, Vanna's hands, the mantle later, crowd in the doorway.
- **Long speeches:** stay on the speaker, then a held listener reaction *after* the line finishes (or intercut only at clause breaks). Do not undercut Sutro with busy cutting.
- **Crowd / Seigniory:** heard and glimpsed (cries of "Vanna, Vanna"; faces afraid at the door). Do not invent a separate council-chamber scene.

## Unit map

Cue lines are Sutro (light OCR cleanup). First = first spoken line of the unit; last = last spoken line before the next unit's first.

### UNIT A — War council at the window
**Players:** Guido, Borso, Torello  
**Action:** Siege fact. Powder gone, last ball fired, fifty-pace breach, desertion if no capitulation tonight. Prinzivalle delayed; Reno murder; Florence's design.  
**First:** GUIDO — *"Our present extremity is so great that the Seigniory have been compelled to reveal to me disasters they had long kept back."*  
**Last:** GUIDO — *"…This is a bitter struggle for life or death; a struggle in which no mercy is shown; in which our wives and our children…"* (broken by Marco's entrance)

### UNIT B — Marco returns
**Players:** Guido, Marco; Borso/Torello present  
**Action:** Embrace. Marco was treated as an honoured guest; Plato / Marsilio Ficino; Commissioners; Prinzivalle delays the assault; Florence already plans his ruin; he will pass a convoy and (if they choose) men into Pisa. Guido wants powder and food, not dangerous auxiliaries.  
**First:** GUIDO — *"Father! … By what happy miracle, what stroke of good fortune in this calamity of ours, have you been restored to us…"*  
**Last:** GUIDO — *"What does he demand in return?"*

### UNIT C — The demand
**Players:** Marco, Guido (lieutenants still present)  
**Action:** Marco stalls, then names the convoy's price: she comes alone, clad only in her mantle, returns at dawn. *"Who?"* — *"Giovanna."* He loves her; she does not remember him; Marco already told her; she turned white and left. Guido moves to the ramparts and death without "dishonour."  
**First:** MARCO — *"The moment has come, my son, when words turn cruel and all-powerful…"*  
**Key:** MARCO — *"…if you will send in exchange and deliver to Prinzivalle — and she shall return with the first rays of dawn — but he demands in token of victory and submission, that she come alone, and clad only in her mantle."*  
**Last:** GUIDO — *"Come, my friends, we will return to the ramparts, and die, at least, since die we must, without staining ourselves with dishonour."*

### UNIT D — Father against son
**Players:** Guido, Marco; Borso, Torello as custody  
**Action:** Duty vs thousands of lives. Marco will tell the people; Guido orders him held. The Seigniory already has the proposal — Marco told them first. Seigniory refused to buy safety with a woman's love and have summoned Vanna. Guido claims *"Vanna is mine."* He accepts her answer in advance as identical to his, or love was a lie.  
**First:** MARCO — *"Ah, Guido, the ordeal is terrible, I know!"*  
**Key:** MARCO — *"They have refused to owe their safety to the sacrifice of a woman's love; and as I left them and hastened to you, they were summoning Vanna, to tell her that they placed in her hands the fate of the city."*  
**Last:** GUIDO — *"…And our love will have been a mere lie, that now crumbles to dust…"*

### UNIT E — She enters and chooses
**Players:** Vanna, Guido, Marco; crowd at the door  
**Action:** Cries of "Vanna, Vanna." She enters alone and pale. Guido embraces her, demands she give Marco words.  
**First (after stage dir):** GUIDO — *"My Vanna! … What have they done, what have they said to you!"*  
**Decision:** VANNA *(approaching Marco)* — *"My father, I shall go to-night."*  
**MARCO** *(kissing her brow)* — *"My daughter, I know."*  
**She states it twice more:** *"Guido, I shall go. I must; I must obey."* / *"I shall go to Prinzivalle's tent to-night."*  
**Last of unit:** GUIDO still trying to rewrite it as assassination or secret love — through VANNA — *"I do not know him; I have never seen him."*

### UNIT F — Refusal and exit
**Players:** Vanna, Guido; Marco, lieutenants, crowd  
**Action:** Guido's jealousy as moral failure: dungeon order (no one obeys), half-drawn sword, *"If your love bids you, Guido."* She: *"I love you… And yet I shall go, I must, I must."* He casts her off. *"Go, I know you no longer."*  
**Last spoken:**  
VANNA — *"Yes."* (to "You go yonder?")  
GUIDO — *"You will not return?"*  
VANNA — *"Yes, I shall return."*  
GUIDO — *"As to that, we shall see. … Who could have told me that my father knew her better than I!"*  
**Button (stage dir, play as image, no extra line):** He totters, clings to a marble column. **Vanna goes out slowly and alone, without another glance at him.** Mantle implication only if she is already wrapped; if not yet, the exit is still clothed-day-into-night threshold — do not generate nude.
