# Act III: The Refusal

**Setting:** Pisa, Guido and Giovanna's residence  
**Time:** Morning, immediately after Act II  
**Principals:** Giovanna, Guido Colonna, Marco Colonna, Vedio (briefly)

## Summary

Morning. Giovanna has returned. Food has arrived — Pisa is saved. But Guido is shattered by doubt and jealousy. He demands to know: **Did Prinzivalle dishonor you?** Giovanna insists: **No. He kept his word. He let me go untouched.** But Guido cannot accept this. He fixates on details, consumed by the image of Giovanna in Prinzivalle's tent. Even if nothing happened, the suspicion poisons his mind. Giovanna, exhausted and furious, refuses to defend herself further: **Believe what you will. I know the truth.** Marco, who initially opposed Giovanna's choice, now defends her, arguing that she saved the city and deserves gratitude, not suspicion. But Guido's pride is wounded beyond repair. He accuses Giovanna of lying or hiding something. Giovanna, dignified but devastated, tells him: **Then you have already lost me.** The play ends with the marriage in ruins — honor saved, but love destroyed. Maeterlinck leaves the ending unresolved: does Giovanna leave? Does Guido beg forgiveness? The final image is moral devastation and ambiguity.
